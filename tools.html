Standard tuning

a chromatic tuner — pitch detection through your microphone, plus reference tones.

  • enable the microphone and play a string; the needle shows how far off you are, in cents — within ±5¢ the display turns green and calls it in tune.
  • no mic handy? tap a string button to hear its reference tone instead.
  • tuned to a = 440 hz, standard tuning. the mic releases when you switch tools.

a metronome that goes well beyond 4/4 clicks.

  • 40–300 bpm with tap tempo — tap along to a recording to match its pace.
  • time signatures from 1/2 to 12/8, four click sounds, and subdivisions from quarters to sixteenths.
  • click any beat to cycle accent → normal → rest; beat settings subdivides individual beats.
  • the white ring marks the beat that's playing, and the run state shows the meter and tempo while it runs.

Song builder

build a chord progression and play along

sketch a chord progression, practice it with backing tracks — and sweep it.

  • pick a key and mode, then click chords from the palette to stack your progression — undo and clear sit beside the grid.
  • drag blocks to reorder, drag their right edge to resize, or use the ‹ › ⧉ × buttons on each block. double-click the ruler to drop section markers; click a flag while playing to jump the loop there.
  • six sounds: warm pad, synthwave, sampled piano, strings, choir, organ — chord changes crossfade seamlessly.
  • bass, arpeggio, and étude tracks play along, each with its own checkbox and level. the étude plays each chord as a sweep arpeggio on piano — see its tab with the “show étude” toggle, where inversions voice-lead from chord to chord. choose 8ths, triplets, or 16ths and a 3–6 string shape.
  • play loop runs 60–200 bpm after a four-click count-in — tap sets the tempo.
  • everything you build is remembered for your next visit.

Scale builder

map notes across the neck

map any scale across the neck and practice it with tab playback.

  • 17 scales, from the modes to pentatonics to exotics. positions are named for the mode that starts there — and with a major-family scale you also get the harmonic minor and phrygian dominant positions that live in the same key.
  • click any note on the neck to hear it. labels switch between fingers, note names, and intervals.
  • a position is always chosen, so the tab is always ready: it walks the shape with a count-in, five instrument voices, tempo, and subdivisions. the frets row lets you widen the window around the position.
  • sequence changes what you practice — intervals in 3rds through 6ths, or sliding groups of 3, 4, and 5, up the shape and back down.
  • loop cycles seamlessly, click adds the metronome, and compare octave shows the same shape an octave away.
  • everything you pick is remembered for your next visit.

Sweep builder

arpeggio sweeps across the neck

arpeggio shapes built for sweep picking — one fluid rake across the strings, up and back down.

  • pick a root, chord quality, string count (3 to 6), and inversion — the shape, tab, and slur markers follow. the shape anchors itself mid-neck from the root you choose.
  • the tab walks the sweep up and back with a bar line at the turn; h and p mark the hammer-on and pull-off that carry the turnaround. loops repeat without doubling the bottom note.
  • six-string shapes top the five-string grip with a lone low-e note on the 5th, like the classic dim7 layout.
  • loop cycles seamlessly at your tempo, with a click track and count-in, in five voices — same transport as the scale builder.
  • diminished and augmented shapes are symmetric: the same shape repeats every 3 (or 4) frets — walk the root up and watch it repeat.
  • major-7 and minor-7 keep the triad patterns and add the 7th — the 5-string minor-7 root is the classic Gambale grip — and carry a fourth inversion with the 7th in the bass.
  • everything is remembered for your next visit.
